# NIH scoring system

Source: NIH, "Scoring System and Procedure",
https://grants.nih.gov/grants/peer/guidelines_general/scoring_system_and_procedure.pdf
(last reviewed 18 March 2015), fetched 2026-11-04. The same 9-point scale, five
scored criteria and averaging rule were in use when this application was
reviewed in February 2013.

For due dates on or after 25 January 2025, NIH reviews R21s and most other
research grants under a simplified framework: Significance and Innovation are
scored together as one factor, Approach as a second, and Investigator(s) and
Environment are judged only as appropriate or not
([NOT-OD-24-010](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-24-010.html)).
This folder uses the older criteria because they are the ones this application
was reviewed against.

## Summary

- One 9-point scale for the overall impact score and for each criterion score. Whole numbers only. Scores of 1 or 9 are expected to be used less often than the others.
- The **overall impact score** is given by every eligible member of the review group. 5 is considered an average score.
- **Criterion scores** are given by the assigned reviewers to at least five criteria (Significance, Investigator(s), Innovation, Approach, Environment).
- The impact score should reflect the reviewer's overall evaluation, not a numerical average of the criterion scores. A reviewer may give moderate criterion scores and a high impact score, or the reverse, when one criterion is critical to the project.
- An application does not need to be strong in all categories to be judged likely to have major impact.
- After the meeting, individual reviewer scores are averaged and multiplied by 10 to give the final impact score, from 10 to 90.
- Applications judged less competitive may be **Not Discussed** (ND). They receive criterion scores but no impact score.

## Score descriptors

| Impact or criterion strength | Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| High | 1 | Exceptional |
| High | 2 | Outstanding |
| High | 3 | Excellent |
| Medium | 4 | Very Good |
| Medium | 5 | Good |
| Medium | 6 | Satisfactory |
| Low | 7 | Fair |
| Low | 8 | Marginal |
| Low | 9 | Poor |

"A score of 5 is a good, medium-impact application. The entire scale (1-9) should always be considered."

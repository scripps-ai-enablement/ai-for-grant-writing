# Research Strategy: Significance

**Significance.** Many studies attempt to use gene expression information to predict the progression of various types of cancer as well as other complex diseases. Such genomic predictors are of interest because, if sufficiently accurate, they could be used to personalize therapy and to cast insights into the molecular underpinnings of disease. Despite extended and intense research in a variety of areas, there are few clinically useful genomic predictors. Of the few that exist, the Oncotype DX® predictor for breast cancer prognosis is among the most widely used [1]. However, its effective application is limited to ER-positive, lymph node-negative tumors and research into the development of more accurate prognostic predictors across all the subtypes remains highly active [2]. As a case in point, in the summer of 2012, ten years after the first major attempts to produce genomic predictors of breast cancer prognosis [3], SAGE Bionetworks launched a large-scale public contest to spur research in this area because suitably accurate predictors still had not been found (http://tinyurl.com/sage-bcc). Though there has unquestionably been progress in the past decade, it has been incremental at best.

We suggest that a fundamentally new methodology is needed to make significant strides on this difficult problem. Since 2002, more than 126,000 publications related to breast cancer have been added to PubMed (http://tinyurl.com/bc-pubs) but, to date, no effective method for integrating the knowledge that this represents with experimental data has been released. In this proposal, we introduce a novel, crowdsourcing-based approach that taps into massive, unused reservoirs of biological knowledge and human intuition. If successful, this contribution will result in significantly better predictors of breast cancer progression and has the potential to make major, disruptive improvements in genomic medicine. Better disease predictors will be adapted to clinical use to personalize and thereby improve patient treatment. They will also prime further basic research into the underlying mechanisms driving the discovered signatures. At a purely methodological level, this work will result in a pattern and toolset for translating genomic data into clinically relevant knowledge using the power of crowdsourcing. In addition to advancing the biomedical research agenda directly, the crowdsourcing mechanism we employ (games) provides an outstanding educational avenue for biologists from related disciplines, for students, and for interested members of the general public.

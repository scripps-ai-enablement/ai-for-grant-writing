# Specific Aims page checklist (built-in)

## Opening paragraph

- The first sentence states the problem and why it matters.
- What is known is summarized briefly, with only the background needed.
- The specific gap in knowledge is named: what we don't know, not just that "more research is needed".
- It closes on the critical need the gap creates.

## Objective and hypothesis paragraph

- The long-term goal is stated.
- The objective of this application is stated.
- The central hypothesis is stated explicitly and is testable.
- The rationale for the hypothesis is given, from published work or preliminary data (preliminary data marked as the author's own).
- The overall approach is given in a sentence.

## The aims

- One to three aims, without sub-aims.
- Each aim has a title stated as an action and is described in two to five sentences.
- Each aim tests part of the central hypothesis.
- The aims are related but not interdependent: if one fails, the others can still proceed.
- For clinical studies, each aim makes clear the population, intervention, comparator, outcomes, time frame and setting.

## Closing paragraph

- States the expected outcomes.
- Says how the results will move the field forward.
- Ties the work to the funder's mission or priorities.
- Links the impact back to the problem in the first sentence.

## The page as a whole

- Fits on one page with room to breathe: no wall of text.
- Jargon is defined once, or avoided, for a reviewer outside the niche.
- Bold and italics are used sparingly, for the sentences a reviewer must not miss.
- The claims are proportionate to the mechanism: no "will prove" or "will cure".

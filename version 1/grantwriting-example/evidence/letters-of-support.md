# Letters of support (summaries)

Four letters, in the order they appear in the application.

## 1. Obi L. Griffith, PhD

- **Position:** Research assistant professor, The Genome Institute, Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis.
- **Date and addressee:** 11 October 2012, to Ben and Andrew. One page.
- **Background given:** Describes his breast cancer work in the labs of Joe Gray and Paul Spellman, and at Washington University, including a predictor of 10-year relapse risk in ER+, LN-, HER2- breast cancer that is being developed into a commercial product, and a study of 70 breast cancer cell lines against 138 agents. Cites three of his own works, listed as in review, a provisional patent application, and submitted.
- **Commitment:** He has "collected and curated a large database of nearly 1400 publicly available breast cancer expression data with clinical outcomes", has "already shared this database with you to use in your prototype game classification games as validation data sets", and "will continue to share future versions as well."
- **Cited in the draft:** Aim 2, Phase 3, as the source of independent test datasets.

## 2. Brian Leyland-Jones, MB BS, PhD

- **Position:** Director, Edith Sanford Breast Cancer, Sanford Research/USD.
- **Date and addressee:** 15 October 2012, to Andrew Su. One page.
- **Background given:** Edith Sanford Breast Cancer's commitment to translational genomic research, its partnership with Scripps Research to sequence thousands of patient tumor samples in the Scripps Genomic core, and his familiarity with games in bioinformatics from a nine-month sabbatical at Stanford University.
- **Commitment:** "I look forward to our working together to evaluate the predictive models of breast cancer recurrence that will be generated by your work", and "we very much look forward to working together to assess your predictive models in breast cancer."
- **Cited in the draft:** Aim 2, Potential problems, "regarding new datasets that we will have advanced access to."

## 3. Josh Peay

- **Position:** Founder, South Bird Studios; formerly Technical Director, Sony Computer Entertainment of America.
- **Date and addressee:** 12 October 2012, to Andrew and Ben. Two pages.
- **Background given:** Fifteen years in the video game industry, from entry-level programmer to technical director on titles in the Socom, Resistance, Ratchet and Clank, and NBA franchises; now produces educational mobile applications and consults for companies. The letter also describes the industry's shift toward small studios and mobile devices.
- **Commitment:** "I look forward to participating in your effort", he considers the idea of hybridizing biological research and video games "a very compelling pursuit and one that I am enthusiastic about mentoring", and he closes "I look forward to our continued interactions in pursuit of building games for science."
- **Cited in the draft:** Aim 1, Phase 1, as the professional game designer to be consulted on the core game mechanic.

## 4. Daniel R. Salomon, MD

- **Position:** Professor; Director, Laboratory for Functional Genomics; Program Medical Director, Scripps Center for Organ Transplantation; Department of Molecular and Experimental Medicine, The Scripps Research Institute.
- **Date and addressee:** 15 October 2012, to Andrew Su. Two pages.
- **Background given:** His involvement in two multicenter NIH U19 programs on kidney and liver transplant patients, and Co-PI role in an NIH Clinical Trials in Organ Transplantation (CTOT) consortium of 6 US transplant centers collecting serial samples from 300 kidney and 300 liver transplant patients over three years.
- **Commitment:** He hopes "that we can use our data to help you test the value proposition created by the success I anticipate in your advancement of the breast cancer prediction challenge in the initial stage", and is "willing to create experiments along the way that we can design together to evaluate your approaches and early results."
- **Cited in the draft:** Aim 1, Potential problems, for a second version of the game on organ transplant rejection.

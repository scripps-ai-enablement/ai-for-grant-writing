## A. Personal Statement

I have led a bioinformatics research group for 10 years in both industry and academia. We apply the tools of statistics, computer science, machine learning, and software engineering to critical problems in biomedical research. Our lab's activities can be broadly separated into two categories: creating tools to accelerate biomedical research, and directly applying those tools to biomedical discovery.

Tools: Our group builds online tools to warehouse, visualize and analyze large scale biomedical data. We have a particular emphasis on harnessing the collective efforts of the biology community toward addressing problems of massive scale. These "community intelligence" initiatives naturally scale with the explosive growth of data generation in science. Our flagship projects in this area include the Gene Wiki (the goal of which is to write a collaboratively-written, community-reviewed, and continuously-updated review article for every human gene), and BioGPS (to crowdsource the development of a comprehensive, extensible, and customizable gene annotation portal).

Biomedical discovery: Our group embraces the data mining challenges that have resulted from high-throughput biology. We have many past and ongoing research projects that span multiple disease areas (from immunology to metabolism to cancer) and multiple biological processes (the unfolded protein response, oxidative stress, and host-pathogen interactions). These projects are based on high-throughput transcriptomics, proteomics, sequencing, genotyping, and phenotyping.

In short, my group has both the technical expertise in web development as well as the bioinformatics expertise to understand the challenges of building disease predictors. Our team is ideally suited to the development of serious games in biology as described in this proposal.

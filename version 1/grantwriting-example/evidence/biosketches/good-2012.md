## A. Personal Statement

The goal of the proposed research is to motivate large numbers of people to contribute their time and mental energy to gene annotation efforts and to the development of classifiers that predict human phenotypes based on molecular data. To do this, we are proposing to translate relevant tasks that would normally be considered work into a series of fun games. By orienting our community incentive structure around fun, rather than financial gain or career advancement, we can dramatically expand the pool of potential workers; however, this translation of work to play is a new kind of challenge that will require a broad and unique collection of skills to accomplish. Specifically, success will depend on the development of novel, enjoyable interfaces for collecting biological knowledge and new techniques for computing effectively with it once collected. Based on my expertise in cognitive science, machine learning, artificial intelligence, bioinformatics and the semantic web, I am uniquely suited to play a leadership role in this project. My undergraduate education in cognitive science provided me with a foundation in both artificial intelligence and human computer interaction design. Early career work developing knowledge-based intelligent systems as well as more recent work with biomedical ontologies gives me a foundation in knowledge-based computing. I developed a solid understanding of machine learning during my Masters degree and as a research associate at UC San Diego. Prior to joining my Ph.D. program, I gained a wide range of experience in bioinformatics, leading projects ranging from the assembly of a genome database for the pathogenic fungus Coccidioides Immitis to the development of sequence-based classifiers for predicting co-receptor usage in HIV viruses. Both as a Ph.D. student and now as a Research Associate, my research has focused on acquiring and validating structured knowledge from large groups of biologists through web-based community intelligence applications. In work published as a graduate student, I developed a novel game-like system for ontology engineering and investigated new systems for open, Web-based document classification. As a post-doc I have successfully lead in efforts to extract ontology-based gene annotations from the text of the Gene Wiki and to motivate the direct contribution of such annotations from the community. In addition I have designed and implemented the first serious game for predicting cancer outcome based on molecular data. Based on more than a decade of professional work in related disciplines I believe strongly in the opportunity presented in the proposed research plan and am prepared to play a vital role in its success.

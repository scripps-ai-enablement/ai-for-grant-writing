## Summary

Numbered most serious first, in the skill's order. First come citations whose source does not support the sentence, then those that need the full text, then details that do not match, then anything not found.

### Cited source does not support the sentence

1. **S9: Foldit's "more than 300,000 players" [12-14].** None of the three supports the figure. [12] is the applicants' own paper, published in December 2011, ten months before submission, and it says "almost 230,000 players". [13] and [14] give no player count in their full texts. The rest of S9, the string of solved challenges, is supported, although [14] also reports where Foldit fell short in CASP9.
2. **S13: the BioGPS audience figures [18].** The 2009 paper reports about 1,500 registered users and almost 10,000 unique visitors per month. The draft's 2 million views a year, 125,000 unique visitors and 5,000 registered users do not appear in it. Whatever the draft's figures rest on, it is not [18], and it is not in evidence/. This sentence is the basis for the recruitment plan in Phase 2, so a panel member who opens [18] will see different numbers.
3. **S15: [5] cited for "improved performance".** Network-Guided Forests performed on par with plain random forests, and on par with NGF run on randomized networks. In the authors' words, "the overall classification performance of NGF does not differ from regular Random Forests". What [5] shows is more reproducible, more biologically focused gene selection. [8] and [20] do support S15.
   - The two sections describe this literature differently. The Innovation says these methods "have made minor improvements in predictive accuracy but yield far more consistent results", with no citation, and that matches [5]. S15 in the Approach says "improved performance".
4. **The Leyland-Jones letter, as summarized, does not mention access to new datasets**, which is what the Aim 2 sentence cites it for. This rests on the summary, so the letter needs checking before acting on it.

### Needs the full text to judge

5. **S6: [7] Bild et al. cited for "pathway databases".** The abstract mentions no database. The secondary descriptions found say the signatures came from cultured cells expressing activated oncogenes. If that holds, [7] is a pathway method built from experiments, not from a database, and does not fit the sentence. There is no PMC copy, so this needs the paper.
6. **S6: [6] Winter et al. cited for "protein-protein interaction networks".** The abstract does not name the network type. Secondary sources say it was a transcription-factor/target network. The full text is open (PMC3355064), and its Methods would settle this.
7. **S8: [10] cited for "collecting common-sense knowledge".** The ESP Game collected image labels. [11] fits the clause and [10] does not, although [10] does support "began". This judgment rests on abstract excerpts, so the paper should be read before deciding.
8. **S2: [2] cited for Oncotype DX's restriction to ER-positive, node-negative tumors.** The abstract does not mention it, and there is no PMC copy.
9. Partial support that matters less:
   - [4]: S5's "consistently" and "secondary and tertiary validation studies" go beyond the abstract.
   - [19]: precedent only.
   - [13] and [14]: they cover the challenges but not the count.
   - Salomon letter: no second game in the summary.
10. **The draft leans hard on several references whose support here comes from abstracts or second-hand excerpts:** [9], [11], [16] and [23], the field-defining citations for games with a purpose and crowdsourcing quality. The same goes for [20] and [21], on which Aim 2's feasibility rests. An abstract that agrees with a sentence is weaker evidence than it looks. Read these before relying on them.
